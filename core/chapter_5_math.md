# Chapter 5: The Sieve & Signal (The $\epsilon$ Equation)

## 🎯 1. Purpose
To mathematically define the relationship between cognitive noise and biological capacity. This module provides a deterministic calculation for the "Sieve" width, allowing the user to predict when the system is approaching a recursive jam ($01$).

## 🔒 2. Canonical Definitions (Symbol Lock)
* **$\epsilon$ (Planck-Ego):** The fundamental ratio of the framework. It represents the "thickness" of the ego-filter. 
* **N (Noise):** The numerator. A count of active cognitive threads, environmental friction ($t+$), and unresolved "Alice" files.
* **C (Capacity):** The denominator. The sum of your morning "Floor" ($C_{base}$) and your real-time Vagal Tone ($RMSSD$).
* **$\alpha$ (Alpha):** A scaling constant (default = 1.0) used to weight the impact of real-time HRV on total capacity.

## 🛠️ 3. Operational Procedure (The "How")

### Step 1: Solving for $\epsilon$
To find your current system state, apply the following formula:

$$\epsilon(t) = \frac{N(t)}{C_{base} + \alpha \cdot RMSSD(t)}$$

1. **Calculate N:** Sum your current tasks + active worries. (e.g., 3 work tasks + 1 physical pain = $N=4$).
2. **Retrieve RMSSD:** Take a 60-second snapshot.
3. **Calculate Ratio:** Divide $N$ by your total capacity.



### Step 2: Regime Identification
Compare your result to the following regimes:
* **$\epsilon < 1.0$ (The Void/00):** You have more capacity than noise. Reality passes through the Sieve effortlessly.
* **$\epsilon \approx 1.0$ (Flow/11):** Perfectly balanced. You are at "Full-Render." High-fidelity work is occurring.
* **$\epsilon > 2.0$ (Overfitting/01):** The "Narrator" is trying to push more data than the hardware can process. The Sieve is clogging.
